Decoding glycogen metabolism &
the diseases it drives.

We define how glycogen metabolism goes wrong in glycogen storage diseases, in the degenerating brain, and in cancer, and turn the mechanisms into pre-clinical drugs and clinical biomarkers.

How we work

Understand → Diagnose → Treat

We elucidate mechanistic understanding of how events at the atomic & molecular levels impact cellular physiology and organismal biology & human disease.

Understand

Structural and enzymological work on laforin, malin, and the machinery that keeps a branched polymer soluble instead of letting it precipitate.

Diagnose

Mass spectrometry imaging and metabolomics that show where glycogen and its metabolites actually sit in tissue — and what is worth measuring in patients.

Treat

Three therapeutic platforms in development: antibody–enzyme fusions, antisense oligonucleotides, and small molecules against aberrant glycogen accumulation.

Research

One molecule, several diseases.

Glycogen sits at a control point in cell metabolism, which is why the same question reaches across conditions that look nothing alike.

Mechanism

Glycogen enzymology

Laforin's glycogen phosphatase activity and malin's E3 ubiquitin ligase activity were both first defined here. We keep working on the carbohydrate-binding modules and regulatory logic that hold the system together.

Disease

Storage disease & neurodegeneration

Lafora disease, Pompe, Cori, and Glut1 deficiency syndrome — plus the aggregate biology that glycogen shares with Alzheimer's disease.

Disease

Cancer metabolism

Ewing sarcoma is an aggressive pediatric bone and soft-tissue cancer, and outcomes remain poor when it spreads or returns. Its cells accumulate unusually large amounts of glycogen, a hallmark noted for decades and never explained. We showed that reducing glycogen strongly suppresses tumor growth, which makes glycogen a therapeutic vulnerability. We are now working out why the tumor needs it, and how to exploit that.

Therapy

Therapeutics & biomarkers

Antibody–enzyme fusions that digest polyglucosan in muscle, heart and brain; antisense oligonucleotides; small molecules; and the biomarkers a trial would need to read out.
